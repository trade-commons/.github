## Global Trade Commons

A neutral home for the shared tools, tests, reference implementations and registries that make the
UN/CEFACT digital trade standards, and the open standards they build on, usable in practice.

The Commons does not write standards. UN/CEFACT owns and governs the normative specifications, with
its secretariat hosted by UNECE; the World Customs Organization governs the WCO Data Model; the W3C
governs the credential and identifier specifications. This organisation holds the implementations,
the tooling, the operated services and the adoption material, and holds no normative authority over
any specification it implements.

Particular attention goes to lowering adoption barriers for developing and emerging economies, so
that the benefits of open digital trade infrastructure reach the economies that would otherwise be
left behind.

### Status

The Global Trade Commons is forming. It is to be constituted as a foundation under the International
Jurisdiction of Digital Commons (IJDC), which the Global Digital Collaboration establishes, expected
in early 2027. The charter is a draft for circulation and has not been executed. Until it is, this
organisation holds preparatory work and nothing here should be read as a commitment of the foundation.

### What is here

The component catalogue at [trade-commons.github.io](https://trade-commons.github.io) is the index.
Components live across four homes: a standards body's own infrastructure for normative artefacts,
this organisation for implementations and operated services, a community's own infrastructure for its
extension and profile, and a vendor's own repositories for its supported products. The catalogue
spans all four, so an entry here often points somewhere else.

### The organising principle

The Commons builds engines. Standards bodies and communities publish the declarations those engines
consume. An Australian livestock passport differs from a Brazilian coffee passport in its schema
extension, its identifier schemes, its validation rules and its vocabulary, and in none of its
software. Code maintained per sector per jurisdiction would make the Commons the bottleneck for every
adoption, and no funded team is large enough for that.

Every engine must work with no community declaration at all, producing a correct if unpolished
result, and improve as declarations arrive.

### Contributing

Contributions come in under the Developer Certificate of Origin. Copyright stays with the
contributor, inbound equals outbound, and no party may relicense the work. There is no contributor
licence agreement and there will not be one, because aggregating copyright is what the charter
forbids.

Code is Apache-2.0, for the express patent grant and defensive termination that matter when competing
vendors contribute to shared infrastructure. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/trade-commons/.github/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) and
[CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](https://github.com/trade-commons/.github/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
